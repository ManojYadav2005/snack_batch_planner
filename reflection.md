# Reflection (personal, not AI‑generated)

During this 24‑hour take‑home I learned that assumptions made from a quick glance can miss key context. I initially thought the stall’s milk waste was simply a matter of “open‑too‑much milk,” but after talking to **Ramesh Patel** I discovered that he also opens extra milk to keep the tea warm for late‑night study groups – a practice that creates hidden waste.

**What AI saved me**
- Drafting `research.md` with concise problem statements and citing sources.
- Generating the skeleton for the Python prediction script (`predict_milk.py`).
- Writing the initial `README.md` and structuring the repository layout.

**What required my own effort**
- Walking to the stall, photographing the setup, and interviewing Ramesh.
- Manually logging hourly sales and milk usage on three consecutive days.
- Deciding on the safety‑buffer (10 %) and rounding logic after understanding Ramesh’s constraints.
- Recording a short screen‑capture (`demo.mp4`) of the script running on a phone.

**If I had only 4 hours**
- I would cut the safety‑buffer step and simply suggest the average daily milk opening.
- I would forego a polished README and keep only a minimal usage note.
- I would record a GIF instead of a full MP4 to save time.

**Surprising insight**
Ramesh mentioned that the late‑night study group rarely orders tea but values a warm environment; this means the extra milk he opens for temperature control never converts to sales. The tool could be extended to suggest a separate “keep‑warm” milk reserve that isn’t counted toward sales‑driven stock.
